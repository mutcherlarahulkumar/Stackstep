## What Stackstep is
keywords: about, what, stackstep, internship, virtual, real job, simulated
Stackstep is a 30 or 60 day virtual internship. You work like you are on a software team: a team lead, a PM and teammates message you in chats, tickets land on your board, you build real projects in your own GitHub repositories, and mentors review your work. It is practice, not a real job. The team is simulated and every automatic reply is labelled, but the work and the reviews are real.

## Signing in
keywords: sign in, login, log in, github, account, sign up, register, password
You sign in with GitHub. There is no separate password. Tap Sign in, approve Stackstep on GitHub, and you come straight back. Your GitHub account is also where your code lives, so use the one you want recruiters to see. If you were signed out by staff or your session expired, just sign in with GitHub again. Your work and chats are kept.

## Applying and paying
keywords: apply, application, cohort, fee, pay, payment, upi, qr, screenshot, transaction, utr, refund, seat
After signing in, pick an open cohort and fill in a short form. Then pay the exact fee by UPI using the QR code or UPI ID shown, and upload the payment screenshot with the transaction ID. We usually confirm within a day. If something is wrong, for example the wrong amount or an unclear screenshot, your application tells you exactly what to fix and you can chat with us there. Fees are non-refundable, so check the cohort and amount before you pay.

## When your internship starts
keywords: start, day 1, first day, tomorrow, begin, when, start date, rolling
Day 1 is the day after you join. You do not wait for a batch start date. Your first task arrives on the morning of Day 1, and your welcome letter in Mail tells you the exact day and time. Every day after that has its own work, set by your flow.

## Your workspace
keywords: workspace, home, screen, layout, panels, where
The workspace has your chats on the left, the open conversation in the middle and your task board on the right. On a phone they are tabs. The header has Mail, your Calendar and your Progress. Opening the workspace each day also marks you present for attendance.

## Chats and personas
keywords: chat, message, persona, team lead, pm, teammate, reply, bot, automatic, real person, reactions, emoji, react
Each persona is a member of your team, such as your team lead or PM. They post standups, assign tickets, nudge you and answer questions. Some replies are automatic and say so with a tag, and mentors read the chats and reply as the personas. You can react to any message with an emoji by hovering it on the web or long pressing it in the app. Messages arrive live while you are online.

## Your task board
keywords: board, task, ticket, todo, to do, in progress, review, done, kanban, status, due, deadline
Tasks move across To do, In progress, Waiting for review and Done. Open a task to read the brief, the review criteria, any reading or lessons, and the deadline. Start it to move it to In progress. Tasks that need changes come back with the reviewer's notes. Finishing on time earns more points.

## Handing in work
keywords: hand in, submit, submission, pull request, pr, link, repository, repo, branch
Most tasks are handed in as a link, usually a pull request in your own GitHub repository. Open the task, paste the link and hand it in. The task moves to Waiting for review and your hand-in is posted in the chat of the persona who assigned it. If a reviewer requests changes, fix them and hand in again from the same task.

## Reviews and feedback
keywords: review, reviewer, mentor, feedback, changes requested, approved, rubric, criteria, rejected
Mentors review your work against the criteria shown on the task, and they quote your actual code. A review either approves the task or requests changes with notes. You get the result in Mail and in the task. Mentors may use AI to draft a review, but a person always decides and sends it.

## Quizzes, readings and lessons
keywords: quiz, reading, lesson, document, markdown, read more, credits, resources, doc reading
Some tasks are quizzes that grade themselves when you submit. Others are readings or lessons that open inside Stackstep with a clean reader, a table of contents, copyable code blocks, credits to the original authors and read more links that open in a new tab. Mark a reading done when you finish it.

## Task notes
keywords: notes, note, private, share, reviewer, markdown notes
Every task has private notes in Markdown. Keep your thinking, commands and links there. When you hand in, you can choose to share your notes with the reviewer.

## DSA practice pad
keywords: dsa, practice, pad, algorithm, problem, leetcode, javascript, tests, run
DSA tasks come with a practice pad. Write your solution in JavaScript and run it against the example tests right in your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere until you hand in.

## AI helper and credits
keywords: ai, helper, assistant, hint, credits, ask ai, groq, explain, stuck, limit
The AI helper gives hints, explains code and reviews your approach without handing you the answer. It asks you to explain the problem and what you tried first. Each question costs credits, there is a daily limit, and new questions have a short cooldown. Credits keep it a helper, not a crutch. If you run out, finish tasks to earn more or ask your team lead.

## Points and progress
keywords: points, progress, score, percent, leaderboard, this week, recap, streak
You earn points for finishing tasks, more when you are on time. Progress in the header shows the share of tasks you have finished, your points, a This week recap and how close you are to the certificate bar.

## Certificates
keywords: certificate, certify, completion, verify, recruiter, code, pdf, download certificate, 70
Finish enough of your work to pass the 70 percent bar and your team lead issues your certificate. Each certificate has a 12 character code and a public page. Anyone can check it from Verify a certificate without an account, so recruiters can confirm it in seconds. You can download it as PDF or PNG.

## Calendar
keywords: calendar, schedule, timeline, week, month, day view, agenda, upcoming, surprise, when does
The Calendar works like Google Calendar with Day, Week, Month and Agenda views. Tasks show up ahead of time at the moment they arrive, live classes show for as long as they run, and a red line marks now. Surprise tasks only show their day, so keep an eye out. Tap anything to open the task or join the class. Tasks ahead stay locked until they arrive.

## Mail
keywords: mail, inbox, letter, announcement, notification, welcome, unread, email
Mail is your in-house mailbox. Your welcome letter, review results, certificates, live class invites and announcements from staff land there. A badge shows unread letters. Letters about a task or a class have a button that opens it.

## Live classes
keywords: live class, class, zoom, meeting, join, screen share, microphone, mic, raise hand, question, q&a, annotate, video
Live classes run in your browser. Join from your invite in Mail, the banner in your workspace or the Calendar. You see the host's shared screen and their drawings on it, hear the class, chat, ask questions in Q&A and upvote others, and raise your hand. If the host lets you speak, your microphone opens. Nothing from the room is recorded or kept after the class ends.

## Attendance and your forest
keywords: attendance, present, absent, missed, seat, forest, tree, streak, marked
Opening Stackstep on a day of your internship marks you present. Your seat number is the order you arrived in that day. Open Progress, then Show attendance, to see every day as a seat (a computer when present, a bare tree when missed) and your forest, where every day present grows a green tree. Drag the forest to turn it around.

## The phone app
keywords: app, android, iphone, ios, apk, download, install, mobile, phone, play store, testflight
Stackstep has an Android and iPhone app with your chats, tasks, calendar, mail and attendance, synced with the web. Get it from the Get the app page. On Android you download the APK and allow the install once. On iPhone you install from the App Store or TestFlight link if there is one, or add the web app to your home screen from Safari. The page also has fixes for common install problems.

## Paused or finished internships
keywords: paused, pause, cancelled, completed, ended, internship over, inactive
If your internship is paused, your workspace says so and new tasks stop until it resumes. Talk to your team lead in chat to sort it out. When your internship ends, your work, chats and certificate stay available.

## Privacy and your data
keywords: privacy, data, terms, delete, personal, information, who sees
We only keep what we need to run your internship: your GitHub profile, your work, chats, reviews and points. Staff can see your work and chats to review and help you. Read the Privacy and Terms pages from the account menu for the details.

## Getting more help
keywords: help, contact, support, problem, bug, broken, not working, stuck, human, talk to someone
If something is not working, first try reloading the page or signing in again. For anything about your tasks, deadlines or reviews, ask your team lead in chat, where a mentor reads it. For payment questions, use the chat on your application. You can also take the tour again from the help button to see where everything is.
