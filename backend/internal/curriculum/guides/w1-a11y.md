# Accessibility from day one

Accessibility means people can use your page whatever their device, eyesight, hands or
attention. Most of it is free if you use the right HTML from the start, and expensive to bolt
on later.

## Start with real elements

The browser gives buttons, links and form fields keyboard support and screen reader names for
free. A `div` with a click handler gets none of that.

```html
<!-- Works with a mouse only -->
<div class="btn" onclick="save()">Save</div>

<!-- Works with mouse, keyboard and screen readers -->
<button type="button" onclick="save()">Save</button>
```

> [!TIP]
> A rule of thumb. If it goes somewhere, it is a link (`<a href>`). If it does something on this
> page, it is a button.

## Give everything a name

- Every image that carries meaning needs `alt` text that says what it shows. Decorative images
  get `alt=""` so screen readers skip them.
- Every form field needs a `<label>` tied to it.
- Icon-only buttons need an accessible name.

```html
<label for="email">Email</label>
<input id="email" type="email" autocomplete="email" />

<button type="button" aria-label="Close menu">
  <svg aria-hidden="true">...</svg>
</button>
```

## Make focus visible and order sensible

People who use a keyboard move with Tab. They must always see where they are, and the order
must follow the page.

```css
:focus-visible {
  outline: 2px solid #7c3aed;
  outline-offset: 2px;
}
```

> [!WARNING]
> Never write `outline: none` without a replacement. It makes your page unusable for anyone on a
> keyboard.

## Structure with headings and landmarks

Use one `h1`, then `h2` and `h3` in order. Wrap areas in `header`, `nav`, `main` and `footer`.
Screen reader users jump between headings and landmarks the way you skim with your eyes.

## Colour and contrast

Body text needs a contrast ratio of at least 4.5 to 1 against its background. Never use colour
alone to show meaning, such as red for an error. Add an icon or words too.

## Try it yourself

1. Unplug your mouse and use your page with Tab, Shift Tab, Enter and Space.
2. Zoom the browser to 200 percent. Nothing should overlap or disappear.
3. Run the Lighthouse accessibility check in your browser's dev tools and fix what it flags.

Write down in your task notes the two problems you found and how you fixed them.
